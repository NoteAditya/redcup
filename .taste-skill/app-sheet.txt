# APP SHEET — Accent ladder
seed 545707579 · drawn 2026-10-29 · kind creator · light
Brief: RedCup brand and creator dashboards for reel bounty campaigns

This is the style for this product. Adopt it whole and keep its move intact; everything the sheet does not fix comes from the
shared defaults in references/app-screens.md. Redraw only for a reason you can state (it contradicts the brand, or the product is
used in a way the style does not suit) and say why in your report. The values were measured on real product screens: use them
as given rather than rounding them towards your habits.

## 1. The style
One brand hue at full strength only on small marks, and its pale tints doing all the area work: the active nav row, a plan card, badges and progress tracks.

**The move:** The full hue goes only on small marks: the one filled-button style, the data series and the active nav icon. Its 50-200 tints carry every area: the active nav row (tint fill with the dark ink of the hue), the plan or trial card, badges and progress tracks. The primary action may instead be near-black, as on the study's newsletter home.

**Colour budget:** 3-10%, nearly all of it as pale tints of the one hue.
**Off-style here:** the full hue on large areas; a second accent family (four families on one screen was flagged in the study); a gradient on the trial chip or upgrade button; a left accent bar on the active nav row on top of the tint (seen once in 23 screens); violet or indigo.
Distilled from 3 screens: a newsletter home, a network analytics overview, a cap-table company home. All three studied screens of this kind used violet, purple or pink as the one hue. The move does not depend on the hue, so the house's violet and indigo ban holds and the library deals a raspberry instead.

**Its face.** Choose one of these (or one of your own of the same weight) before you build, write it in your notes next to
the screen's question, and check that it shows on the first screen. Without one the screen reads as a well-applied kit.
- The ladder built from the brand's own hue (its logo, the brief's object) in place of the dealt raspberry: the full hue on small marks, its pale tints on areas, never violet or indigo.
- The product's objects in the lead block: the newsletter's latest issue as its cover, the shop's products as 40px thumbnails, the course's lessons with their own art.
- A ground one level toward the hue's palest tint (#fbf7f8 for the raspberry) in place of #f9fafb, so the page and the tints read as one family.

## 2. Tokens
Kept from the brand: map its colours onto this style's roles (ground, surface, raised, border, rule, control, text, muted, accent, status pills, chart) and keep its proportions: 3-10%, nearly all of it as pale tints of the one hue. Take the sizes, radii, row and control heights from the style as they are.

## 3. Type — DM Sans
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com"><link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.gstatic.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=DM+Sans:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
`font-family: "DM Sans", Inter, system-ui, sans-serif`. Weights 400, 500, 600, 700. Sizes: title 32px · section 18px · kpi 24px · body 14px · small 12px. 700 for the page title only.
Numbers: `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every table, KPI and anything that updates. Sentence case; no tracked capitals.

## 4. Shell — Open plane
The labelled sidebar sits on the page ground with nothing or one hairline between it and the canvas; there is no top bar, and the page header and cards sit straight on the ground about 32-36px from the top. Workspace or account switcher at the top of the sidebar, primary nav on a 34px pitch, groups under 12-13px sentence-case grey labels with 22-56px of air above, utilities and the user row pushed to the bottom. Cards are white on the off-white ground (or the sidebar is white with a 1px border on a #f9fafb canvas). The canvas is fluid with one gutter value, or a centred column for personal homes.
Numbers: sidebarWidth 230-256 (230, 240, 256 in the study; 280 and 292 with a built-in icon rail); separation none, or one 1px hairline; the sidebar sits on the page ground or within 1-2% of it; navPitch 34; navRowHeight 32; navIcon 16px outline, about 1.5px stroke; navLabel 14px, 400, grey or ink; activeItem neutral grey pill, radius 6-12, inset 8-12px from the sidebar edges (#e9e9e9-#ededed); topBar none; canvas fluid with a 24-64px gutter, or a centred 1100px column.
On a phone: The sidebar folds into a 48px top bar holding the screen's name, a menu button that opens a full-screen sheet with the nav and the switcher, and at most one action. Content takes the full width with 16px margins.

## 5. Screens

### overview — KPI strip, main chart and a side rail
Title row: a 24px noun or the product's name at 600-700, an optional 14-16px grey line under it, and the one primary action on the right, bottom-aligned with that line. 16-24px below, the scope row: section tabs and the date range in one row of 32-36px controls, or a 48px segmented track with the range select inside its right end. Then a grid of 1fr and a fixed 384px rail with a 24px gap. Main column: one KPI container split by 1px dividers into 3-5 equal cells (14px grey label, 24px value at 400-600, a 12px caption naming the baseline and window), then the main chart card, which is the largest thing on the screen, then the next card, all 16px apart. Rail: 16px headings outside their cards, 8px above each and 16px after the previous card; a recent-items list of 3-5 rows on a 45px pitch with 1px rules, closed by a 30px outlined 'View all' button instead of pagination. The rail starts level with the first card, not with the title.
On a phone: The rail moves below the main column; the KPI container becomes a 2-column grid; tabs scroll inside their own row.

## 6. Always
Real data for this product, written before the markup: names, amounts that add up, dates in order, units on every figure,
honest zeros and dashes. One chart answers one question. No gradients, glows, glass, icons in tinted squares, sparklines on KPIs,
rainbow charts, emoji, lorem or Acme. `<skill>/assets/app.css` and `<skill>/assets/charts.js` are a neutral start set to these
rules. Check with `node <skill>/scripts/lint.mjs <files>` and `node <skill>/scripts/inspect.mjs <page>`: this sheet switches both
to app rules.
