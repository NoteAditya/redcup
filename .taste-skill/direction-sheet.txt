This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Take one shape from the subject and let it run the whole system. A drilling tool's octagonal cross-section becomes the buttons, the image masks and the loader. A two-account money app becomes two circles that recur as icon, inline dot and section colour. A café's striped awning becomes every band on the page. Look at the product, the logo, the object or the word in the headline and find the simplest geometry in it (a circle, an arc, a chamfer, a stripe, a notch, a ring). Then use only that shape, at three or four very different scales, everywhere a designer would normally reach for a generic rounded rectangle.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: the motif appears once at enormous scale as a flat filled shape, cropped by the viewport (a circle 120vh across entering from the right, a chamfered block filling the lower half), with a short headline at 6–8vw and one supporting sentence set on the remaining ground, left-aligned on a shared margin. Through the page the motif returns as: the mask for every photograph (via `clip-path` or `border-radius` made from the same geometry), the shape of the primary button, the bullet beside list items at text size, and one full-width band that separates the two halves of the page. Sections use simple two-column or single-column layouts so the shape is the only thing with character.

## 3. Type — Chillax
A soft geometric sans with wide round letters and relaxed joins, warm at semibold and easy at text size. Suits consumer apps, community products, cafes and small friendly brands.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=chillax@400,500,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Chillax** — weight 600 · tracking -0.03em · leading 1.0 · sentence case
- Text: **Chillax** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A clean geometric or grotesk face that echoes the shape (round bowls for circles, squared terminals for chamfers). Medium weight; headings 6–8vw in the hero and 3–4vw after.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. The motif carries the accent colour at large scale, flat. Everything else is ground and ink. A second motif colour is allowed only if the subject has a real pair (two accounts, two modes).

## 5. Imagery
**This subject is software and there are no screenshots, so its pictures are its own interface.** Wherever this sheet says photograph, image or object, build a believable fragment of the product in HTML at real size (real labels, numbers that add up, one item hovered or selected) by the app-screen rules (references/app-screens.md: deal its style once with `node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --app --kind <kind> --keep palette,type --out .taste-skill/app`, and build it with assets/app.css and assets/charts.js), put the attribute `data-ui` on its root, and crop or scale it the way the blueprint treats a photograph: a full-bleed close crop, a tile, the fixed half of a split. Where a fragment makes no sense, use a flat colour field with type on it. Never a fake dashboard of grey bars, never a device frame. If the product works on something physical (ships, plants, buildings, a city), one real picture of that world can carry a section: find it with `node /workspace/om-app/app/.agents/skills/design-taste-frontend/scripts/assets.mjs "<that world>"`, download it with `--get`, credit it on the page, and never let it pass for the product or its customers.

Photographs appear inside the motif's shape. If there is product interface to show, it sits on a flat colour stage shaped by the motif. Draw the shape with CSS or a simple inline SVG path; do not draw illustrations.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

Optional atmosphere, two at most, from the "Architecture" set (structure, lines, bridges, facades and building interiors). The page does not need them.
Order of preference for imagery: (1) the project's own photographs and product shots; (2) images you generate, if an image tool is
available; (3) real pictures of the subject's world found in the open collections (assets.mjs, downloaded and credited on the page);
(4) drawings of the subject in code; (5) these. They are real photographs (Unsplash licence, served by picsum.photos) of places, light and
texture. They are atmosphere, never a stand-in for a specific product, dish, person or interface. List the ones you use in your report so
they can be replaced.

## 6. Hero — Fragment on a shaped stage
A neutral ground. The headline occupies the upper-left third: two lines at clamp(52px, 5.2vw, 80px), medium weight, flush left on columns 1–6 at a 40px margin, starting 150px down. One supporting sentence at 18px, max 40ch, follows, then the single action as a small rectangular button. The rest of the screen belongs to one flat stage in a saturated colour: it spans columns 5–12, begins 42% down, and bleeds off the right and bottom edges. Its top-left corner is cut with the product's own shape using `clip-path` (a notch, a chamfer or a tab two columns wide), the only decoration on the page. On the stage, 56px in from its left and top edges, sits the main fragment of the interface at true size with labels at 13–15px, built in HTML and working: one panel or control about 620px wide, cropped by the bottom edge, never a whole window and never inside a browser or laptop frame. Mark its root with data-ui. Navigation is a plain row at 28px from the top: wordmark left and four text links right at 15px. Nothing overlaps the headline.

On a phone: The headline sits under the wordmark at 10vw on two lines, followed by the sentence at 16px and the button. The stage comes next at full width, bleeding off both sides and the bottom, its corner cut kept at 72px wide. The fragment sits 16px inside it at true size; if it is wider than the screen the right edge crops it rather than scaling it down.
Headline: A plain statement of what the product does in three to six words, two lines of up to about 16 characters each at 5.2vw.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. how-it-works — Many into one (band)
A diagram drawn with one repeated shape instead of arrows. The heading (two to four words on two lines at clamp(36px, 4vw, 60px)) and a short paragraph at 17px sit in columns 1–4. Beneath, a wide inline SVG spans columns 1–12 at about 4.5:1. It is built from 120–160 small filled squares (or the page's own motif), each 10–14px. In the left third they are scattered loosely in two pale tones. In the middle third they are pulled into a tight horizontal ribbon three squares high. In the right third they fan out again into neat, evenly spaced rows in the accent colour. The eye reads disorder, compression, order, from left to right. Under the diagram, three text columns aligned to its thirds: a bold one-word name at 20px and two lines at 16px each; the words carry the sequence ('Gather', 'Reduce', 'Release'). No connecting marks.
On a phone: The diagram turns to run top to bottom: a 3:4 SVG with the scatter at the top, the ribbon as a vertical band in the middle and the ordered rows at the bottom. The three names and their text sit to its right in a 38vw column, each level with its zone.

### 2. stats — Stepped figure tiles (grid)
Three figures on three tiles that refuse to line up. A tall tile in the accent colour fills columns 1–6 and is 70vh high; it holds one figure at clamp(56px, 5.4vw, 92px) in its top-left corner, 28px in, and its caption at 17px in its bottom-left corner, with empty colour between. On the right, an ink-coloured tile in columns 7–9 starts 14vh lower and is 44vh high: a paper-coloured figure top-left, a caption along its bottom edge. A third tile in a mid tone takes columns 10–12, starts 26vh down and is 34vh high, with the same internal layout. Gaps are 8px. The unequal heights leave notches of bare ground above and below the right-hand tiles; the heading, two to four words at 28–36px, sits in the upper notch on column 7. If the page has a motif, cut one corner of each tile to its geometry with clip-path.
On a phone: The tall tile comes first, at full width and 56vw high, with its figure at 15vw. The two smaller tiles follow side by side with a 6px gap, the right one starting 32px lower than the left, each with its figure at 11vw and its caption at the bottom. The heading sits above at 26px.

### 3. list — One item per poster line (statement)
A short list set as a poster. Four to seven items (services, headline acts, courses, opening days) stack one per line at clamp(64px, 10vw, 150px) in the display weight, all capitals or all lowercase, leading 0.92, flush left at a 20px margin on a flat field. Its one detail (a price, a date, a time range) sits at the right margin on the same baseline at 18–20px with tabular figures, so the page has two alignments only: giant words left, small facts right. Items too long for one line at this size are shortened, not shrunk. Between items there is only the tight leading. Hovering an item swaps its ink for the accent. There is no heading: the first item starts 80px below the section top and the last ends 80px above the bottom. The list reuses the hero title's exact type size, so it reads as another poster.
On a phone: Items at 15vw, one per line, flush left with 16px margins. Each detail moves beneath its item at 15px, left-aligned, with 6px above and 22px below. If any single word is wider than the screen at 15vw, drop the whole list to 12vw rather than breaking a word.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Chillax, text Chillax) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

### 4. showcase — Object on a disc (index)
One section about a viewport tall on a plain ground. A flat filled disc 64vh across, in one palette colour, sits with its centre at 58% of the width; swap the disc for the subject's own shape if it has one. A cut-out object photograph stands in front of it, centred on it, about 78% of the disc's height and breaking the disc's outline at the top. Bottom-left, starting on the margin and ending level with the disc's lower edge: a vertical list of four to six item names at clamp(28px, 2.9vw, 44px), leading 1.15. The selected name is in full ink and the rest at 25%; choosing a name swaps the object (300ms crossfade) and the disc's colour. The heading, two to four words at the same size as the names but in the soft ink, is the first line of that list. One fact about the selected item sits at the disc's right edge at 17px.
On a phone: The disc comes first, 86vw across and centred, with the object in front. The names follow as a single sideways-scrolling line at 26px with 24px gaps, the selected one in full ink. The fact sits under the names at 16px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.

### 5. faq — Tabbed index cards (stack)
Questions are filed like cards in a drawer. A heading of two to four words at about 4vw sits in columns 3–10. Beneath it, in the same columns, five or six filled panels are stacked so that each overlaps the one above, leaving 96px of every card showing. Each card has a tab on its top edge, 200px wide and 40px tall, stepping right by 150px per card, cut with `clip-path: polygon()` so card and tab are one filled shape. The cards rotate through two or three palette colours. The tab carries the topic in one or two words at 16px; the visible strip under it shows the question at 20px medium. Choosing a card expands it to show the answer at 17px over at most 50ch and pushes the cards below it down. One card is open at a time; the first is open on load.
On a phone: Cards run full width with the same overlap, 88px of each showing. Tabs shrink to 120px wide and step right by 48px per card. The open card expands in place with its answer at 16px and 20px padding, and the heading leads at 9vw.

### 6. cta — A sentence with a blank (statement)
The closing sentence has a gap in it, and the gap is the form. A sentence of five to eight words is set at about 6vw on two lines, flush left across columns 1–11; there is no separate heading. Where the reader's email (or name, or city) belongs, an `<input>` sits inline on the baseline: a filled block of the one-step tone, exactly one line-height tall and about 7em wide, with the placeholder in the same face and size in the softer ink, no border, and the page's radius or motif corner. The sentence's last word is the submit `<button>`, set in the same type on a block of the accent colour of the same height. Field and button use `em` units so they scale with the sentence. One line at 16px under the sentence, in the softer ink, says what happens after sending. Nothing else is in the section.
On a phone: The sentence is set at 10vw on two lines; the input drops to a line of its own between them at full width and one line-height tall, and the final word, as the button, ends the second line. The explaining line follows at 15px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 7. footer — The mark, built large (band)
On a flat colour field, the brand closes the page as a construction rather than as set type. Across the full content width and about 34svh tall, the wordmark is built from blocks: each letter is knocked out of its own solid rectangle in a palette colour, the rectangles differ in height (60–100% of the band) and butt together with 4px gaps, bottoms on one line. Where the project has a simple geometric mark or motif, enlarge that instead, drawn as inline SVG from flat rectangles, circles and quarter-arcs with the same 4px gaps. Below, 56px lower: four columns of plain links at 15px across columns 1–8, and two addresses or contact lines in columns 9–12. A last row carries the copyright at the left and one small filled button at the right. Space and the block construction do all the separating.
On a phone: The block wordmark spans the full width at about 22svh tall; a long name breaks into two rows of blocks. Link columns go two across at 15px, contact lines beneath at full width, then copyright and the small button on one row.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
One shape at many sizes. The giant cropped shape gives the hero mass and colour without a single image.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
One motif means one. A circle here, a hexagon there and a blob behind is the generated look this replaces. The motif must come from the subject and you must be able to say from where in one clause.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
